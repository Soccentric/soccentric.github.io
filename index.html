Why this exists

I'm Sandesh Ghimire, an embedded software and firmware engineer with 25 years of experience, the last 10 as a consultant.

On every project I kept rebuilding the same foundations from scratch: image build, board bring-up, configuration, device management, testing. Weeks of work before the real product could even start.

So I built those foundations once, properly, as reusable building blocks, and ported them across 10 processor families: same architecture, same tools, same workflow, whether it's a Raspberry Pi, a Jetson, an i.MX or an STM32.

Weeks of groundwork become days.On every project I start

What you get

A proven base, so your team works on your product rather than the plumbing.

A fast start

A working image, configuration and device tooling on your board from day one.

Shorter time to project

Bring-up and groundwork that usually take weeks are done in days.

No reinventing the wheel

The same proven pieces, reused across boards, chips and products.

Used on real projects. These building blocks are my starting point on every current consulting engagement. They are not a demo: they are how I work.

Why open source

Not every company has the people or budget to build this groundwork themselves. Releasing it means small teams and startups can get a working base on their board quickly and spend their time on their own product.

Who it's for

  • Startups and small teams bringing up embedded Linux or MCU products
  • Teams comparing processors who want the same stack on each
  • Engineers who want a proven starting point instead of a blank repository
